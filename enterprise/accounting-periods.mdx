---
title: 'Accounting periods'
description: 'Mark closed months in your close process, and see how period status is recorded'
---

When your finance team closes a month, record that close on the accounting period. The period status is the sign-off point for your close process.

## Close a period

```bash
curl -s -X POST "$ORVLIN_API/v1/accounting/periods/9/2026/lock" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_SESSION_TOKEN"
```

The path takes the month as `:month` (1 to 12) and the year as `:year`. The example closes September 2026. The response is the updated period, with `status: "closed"` and `closed_at`.

## Reopen a period

Reopen a period only for corrections, and record the reason in your own change log.

```bash
curl -s -X POST "$ORVLIN_API/v1/accounting/periods/9/2026/unlock" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_SESSION_TOKEN"
```

The response shows `status: "open"`.

## What the status means today

- The period's status is recorded as `closed` or `open`. Your reporting and close checklists should read it before finalising figures.
- The status does not yet stop changes to a closed period through the API. Check the status before you make corrections to a period.

<Warning>
Until enforcement is in place, the status is a record of your close, not a lock. Treat changes to closed periods as exceptions, and log them.
</Warning>

## Recommended close process

1. Confirm all usage for the month has been ingested. Late events are the most common cause of surprises.
2. Review the revenue summary: `GET /v1/revenue/summary`.
3. Close the period.
4. Export the audit log for the period, and store it with your close file.
