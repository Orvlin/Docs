---
title: 'Accounting periods'
description: 'Lock closed months so billing data for them can't change'
---

When your finance team closes a month, lock its accounting period. Locked periods protect the figures your books depend on, so a late usage event or a re-run can't quietly change a closed month.

## Lock a period

```bash
curl -s -X POST "$ORVLIN_API/v1/accounting/periods/9/2026/lock" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_SESSION_TOKEN"
```

The path takes the month as `:month` (1 to 12) and the year as `:year`. The example locks September 2026.

## Unlock a period

Unlocking is for corrections. Record the reason in your own change log, because the unlock is also audited.

```bash
curl -s -X POST "$ORVLIN_API/v1/accounting/periods/9/2026/unlock" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $ADMIN_SESSION_TOKEN"
```

## What locking does

A locked period is closed in the accounting ledger, so changes that would alter its figures are blocked. Lock and unlock actions are recorded in the [audit log](/enterprise/audit-logs).

<Note>
Before your first close, test the lock in sandbox with a test clock, and confirm which changes it blocks in your workspace.
</Note>

## Recommended close process

1. Confirm all usage for the month has been ingested. Late events are the most common cause of surprises.
2. Review the revenue summary: `GET /v1/revenue/summary`.
3. Lock the period.
4. Export the audit log for the period, and store it with your close file.

<Tip>
Lock the period only after your revenue summary matches your expectations. Unlocking is possible but leaves a trail.
</Tip>
